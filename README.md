## 📖 Overview

**NAME:** is an adaptive reasoning companion that speaks the language of everyday argumentation. Where the original *Logical Fallacy Trainer* planted a flag on the hill of spotting fallacies, **NAME:** builds the entire mountain range around it — a sprawling ecosystem for sharpening the way you think, question, and reason in real time.

Think of it as a flight simulator for your intellect. You don't just read about cognitive biases; you sit in the cockpit, face live scenarios, and get scored on how gracefully you navigate turbulence in reasoning.

This repository houses the core engine, the scenario library, the multilingual reasoning modules, and the community-contributed case sets. It is designed for educators, debate coaches, self-learners, psychology enthusiasts, and anyone who wants to feel the friction of a well-built argument.

> "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself — and you are the easiest person to fool." — A sentiment woven into every module here.

## ✨ Why NAME: Exists

Most tools tell you *what* a fallacy is. **NAME:** lets you *feel* one happening — inside a conversation, inside a headline, inside your own drafting notes. It is less a dictionary and more a dojo.

- You learn by doing, not by scrolling.
- You get immediate counter-explanation, not silent judgment.
- You build a personal "reasoning fingerprint" over time.

## 🗺️ How It Works (A Bird's-Eye View)

NAME: revolves around a simple loop:

1. **Present** — A scenario appears, written to feel human, not textbook.
2. **Probe** — You tag the reasoning slip you suspect.
3. **Pivot** — You get a counter-explanation and, optionally, a rewrite.
4. **Progress** — Your fingerprint updates, and the next scenario shifts accordingly.

Over weeks, this loop becomes a habit. And habits become instincts.

## 🌍 Multilingual Support in Practice

NAME: doesn't simply swap words. It respects idiom. A sarcastic tweet in one language is rewritten with equivalent bite in another. The goal: learners experience reasoning in their own cultural texture.

Currently supported locales include (with more arriving each quarter):

- Japanese
- Korean
- Hindi

Each locale has its own scenario set, reviewed by native speakers and reasoning coaches.
